# Judge dimension: reference quality

The first images show candidate figures. The remaining images show reference figures.
The first reference shows the diagram type. Later references supply supporting design examples.
Compare the images directly before you score.
Write the reason in Simplified Technical English.

Score 1 only when the candidate meets all relevant conditions for its diagram type:

- The composition explains the requested question with a clear hierarchy.
- The groups express real responsibilities, boundaries, or related entities.
- The visual notation distinguishes the relevant roles, actions, decisions, outcomes, or relationships.
- The routes remain traceable across the complete figure.
- The figure preserves the necessary detail without dense prose or arbitrary empty regions.
- The labels remain readable at the requested final width.

Score 0 when the candidate has a material deficit against its diagram type.
A generic chain of identical boxes does not pass for a system with distinct roles and boundaries.
A list of table fields does not pass without organized relationships and cardinalities.
A workflow does not pass when prose replaces its decisions, branches, or outcomes.
Correct text and successful compilation do not establish this quality.

Judge the composition and visual notation, not a preferred palette, renderer, or exact node count.
Allow fewer elements when the actual question requires fewer elements.
Do not require the candidate to copy unrelated facts from a reference.
Use `unknown: true` when the candidate or reference images do not permit a visual comparison.
